Running it on your own customers.

Today the engines run on one fixed file: IBM's Telco sample, a single snapshot of 7,043 customers. This page sets out the format a customer file would need for the app to run on it instead, including month-by-month history, and what changes in each engine's output when it does.

Snapshot template (.csv) Monthly template (.csv) Both open in Excel. Excel workbooks (.xlsx) would be accepted too.

Two layouts

One row per customer, or one row per customer per month

Layout A · snapshot

One row per customer

The customer as they are today: how long they have been a customer, whether they have left, and their current plan. This is the layout of the IBM file, so the app's results on it are the reference.

  • Simplest to export from a billing or CRM system
  • Monthly churn risk is reconstructed from tenure
  • Cannot show customers who changed plan, or prices that changed
Layout B · monthly history

One row per customer per month

Every month each customer was active: what they had, what they paid, and whether they left that month. The better choice when the history exists.

  • Monthly churn risk is fitted directly on customer-months
  • Follows customers who change services, contract or payment
  • If list prices changed during the history, price sensitivity can be estimated (see below)

Optional extra sheets

Data that replaces an assumption with a measurement

Three of the app's inputs are assumptions today, because the IBM file cannot measure them. Each extra sheet would let the app estimate one of them from your data instead.

prices

List prices by month

month, service, list_price

  • Prices are read directly instead of recovered from the bills
  • If prices changed during the history, price sensitivity is estimated, with a range
offers

Retention offers made

customer_id, month, offer, accepted

  • Acceptance rate measured per offer instead of the 30% prior
  • Offer effects estimated against similar customers who were not offered, most reliably if offers were assigned at random
costs

Unit cost per service

service, monthly_unit_cost

  • A margin per service instead of one 70% margin for everything
  • Engine 1 then trades off services by each service's own margin

Engine 1 · prices

A different price list, possibly a different shape

  • The starting point is your current prices and your current average churn, which becomes the churn ceiling.
  • The pattern on the IBM data (add-ons +25%, fibre −17.9%) is a result for those customers, not a rule: which price gets cut depends on your customers' services and churn.
  • With an estimated price sensitivity, the result is shown at the estimate and across its range, instead of across 0.4-1.5.
  • The value of 1% more churn room is recomputed for your customer base.
Engine 2 · calls

A different call list and offer mix

  • Candidates are your customers with a monthly churn risk of 3% or more, so the list's size follows your churn level.
  • Who qualifies for which offer follows your contracts, services and payment methods.
  • With measured acceptance and offer effects, the robustness check stresses those estimates' ranges instead of the fixed ×0.5 / ×0.25 and 15% / 50% cases.
  • Expected value scales with your bills and margins.
Coupling

A different budget curve

  • Re-run on your data, the sweep shows where retention spend stops freeing pricing room for your customers.
  • The IBM curve flattens after about $30,000; yours will bend where your churn ceiling stops binding.
Unchanged

What stays the same

  • The two optimisation models and their solvers.
  • The rules: price bounds, bill-shock cap, churn ceiling, budget, hours and caps (all still adjustable).
  • The pages, the shared settings and the background jobs.

Before the engines run

Checks a file would have to pass

Every file

  • Every required field is matched to a column
  • No duplicate customers (layout B: no duplicate customer-months)
  • Values are within the allowed lists; numbers are numbers
  • Bills are explained by the services held (a warning, not an error, if the fit is poor)
  • A report lists every row that was skipped and why

Monthly history (layout B)

  • Months run without gaps from a customer's first month to their last
  • churned_this_month is yes at most once, on the last row
  • Enough customers leave for the churn model to be fitted (a minimum number of churn events)
  • Price sensitivity is estimated only if list prices actually changed

Your customers' data

Customer files are personal data. The intended design processes an upload in memory for that session and never stores it. On a public demo server, uploads would be switched off; running the app locally or in its Docker container keeps the data on your own machine.
